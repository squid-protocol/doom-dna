# DOOM demo studies: plan and Phase 0 pilot (2026-11-25; not registered)

**Idea.** DOOM records inputs as demos (`.lmp`) that replay tic by tic. A faithful port stays in sync; a divergent one desyncs. That is a behavioural ground truth to set against our structural measures (fingerprints, call graph, the finder tiers).

## Studies
1. **Structure to sync:** does the retained structure of the playsim subsystems predict sync with a vanilla-compatible engine, and where does the first desync fall?
2. **Performance:** `-timedemo` speed and per-function profile joined to the matched pairs (performance inheritance).
3. **Behavioural truth for matching:** per-function call counts under the same demo, as a name-free, text-free truth for translations (replaces E12's weakest truth).
4. **Swap tests:** replace a matched function by its counterpart and replay: does sync survive? (the real accuracy of each tier).

## Phase 0 gate (why a pilot comes first)
If every close port syncs perfectly there is nothing for study 1 to explain, and the registered effort should go to studies 2 and 3. So Phase 0 measures how much sync varies across ports before anything is registered.

## Harness (this branch, `demo/harness/`)
* `e13_headless.c`: headless platform layer for doomgeneric-family engines; writes one digest line per game tic: `gametic leveltime prndindex rndindex x y z angle health armor weapon ammo0..3`. A fake clock keeps timing deterministic; the demo runs under `-timedemo -nodraw -nosound -nomusic -nogui`.
* `build_doomgeneric_family.py`: copies a pinned clone to a work directory (the clone is never modified), inserts one call after the per-tic game-time increment, compiles. `e13_pure_main.c` + `build_puredoom.py` do the same for PureDOOM.
* Third-party source is never committed: only our driver and the patcher (which inserts one line at a short anchor). Builds and digests live outside the repo (`doom-e7-work/demo-study/`).
* Compare on `leveltime, prndindex, x, y, z, angle, health, armor, weapon, ammo`. **Do not compare `rndindex`**: it is the cosmetic RNG (status-bar face, sound) and differs between engines for harmless reasons.

## Phase 0 results (2026-11-25; pilot, not registered)
**Engines built headless with the digest (owner approved installing missing dependencies):** `chocolate-doom` (reference), `doomgeneric`, `crispy-doom`, `prboom-plus`, `sdl-doom`, `inter-doom` (all C, SDL2 dummy video/audio or the doomgeneric headless layer) and `puredoom`. Shareware `DOOM1.WAD` (md5 `f0cefca49926d00903cf57551d901abe`).
* **The harness checks out:** two independent builds of the chocolate family and `doomgeneric` give byte-identical digests; repeat runs are identical.
* **Shareware demos 1 to 3 (5,026 / 3,836 / 2,134 tics):** six engines are identical to the reference on every compared field at every tic. `puredoom` (linuxdoom 1.10 based) matches for 346 tics on demo 1 and then diverges (weapon field first); it cannot load external demo files, so demos 2 and 3 were not played. Plausible cause (not established): the released 1.10 source differs in behaviour from the shipped 1.9 executable.
* **Random self-play demos:** `demo/harness/gen_demos.py` writes vanilla-format demos from a seeded random policy (run, strafe, turn, fire, use, weapon switch; our own inputs, no third-party content). 300 demos, 4,000 tics each, all maps E1M1 to E1M9, skills 1 to 4: **1,800 of 1,800 runs identical across the six engines** (`demo/phase0_random_demo_matrix.csv`). The player moved and fired in every demo and died in 187, so the demos exercise combat and death, not just walking.
* **Boom-family caveat:** `prboom-plus` and `inter-doom` do not keep the gameplay RNG index in every mode (or keep it private), so they are compared on the game state without that column.

## First translation: Mocha Doom (Java), same demos, same reference
Built with `javac` from a patched copy (`demo/harness/build_mochadoom.py`, our `E13Digest.java`); runs under a virtual display.
* **Shareware demos 1 to 3:** identical to the reference on every field at every tic except the **view angle for 3, 9 and 25 tics while the player is dead** (the death-camera turns toward the attacker by a different step; it lands on the same angle). Position, health, armor, weapon, ammo and the gameplay RNG index match throughout.
* **300 random self-play demos** (`demo/phase0_mochadoom_vs_chocolate.csv`): strictly identical on every field in 130; identical on gameplay (angle ignored while dead) in **291**; **9 real gameplay desyncs** (3%). In each, the gameplay RNG index drifts by one call (once two, once 23) at some tic and the player's position diverges 2 to 122 tics later (one demo shows only the RNG drift). They are not spread evenly: 5 of 29 demos on E1M5 (17%) against 4 of 271 on the other maps (1.5%); small numbers.
* So a Java translation of a C program is behaviourally identical on the player's trajectory in 97% of random play, and identical on the shareware demos apart from a cosmetic camera. Not yet known: which Java function consumes a different number of random numbers (a call-by-call RNG trace on one of the nine demos would show it).
* Deliberately a post hoc rule: "ignore the angle while the reference player is dead" was set after seeing the first demo; the strict numbers are reported alongside.

## Second translation: gore (Go, a translation of doomgeneric)
Go 1.23.4 installed in the private tools directory; built headless from a patched copy (`demo/harness/build_gore.py`, `gore/e13digest.go`, a no-op frontend). gore reads files relative to the working directory.
* **Not behaviourally equivalent.** On the 300 random demos **300 of 300 diverge in gameplay** (view angle ignored while dead); the first divergence is at a median of tic 6 (quartiles 2 to 30; 270 of 300 within the first 100 tics). On the shareware demos it plays all tics but diverges at tics 6, 56 and 3 (gameplay RNG), with health or position drifting by tic ~108 to 183. (`demo/phase0_gore_vs_chocolate.csv`.)
* **Root cause #1, found by tracing.** A call-by-call trace of the gameplay RNG (caller name per call; `E13_TRACE_BUILD`) showed monsters acting at tic 6 in gore and not in the C engine; a trace of the line-of-sight function showed identical inputs (same monster, same positions) giving "can see" in gore and "cannot" in C in 37 of 838 distinct cases. The cause is in `p_CrossSubsector`: the loop counter `i` is declared but the body indexes `segs[sub.Ffirstline]`, so only the first wall of each subsector is ever tested (the C code advances a pointer with `seg++`). A **one-token change** (`segs[i]`) in a work copy moves the median first divergence from tic 6 to tic 104 (146 of 300 within 100 tics), a 17-fold delay; it does not remove the divergence.
* **More bugs remain** (e.g. a different shot/blood-spawn sequence at tic 85 of demo 1; a skipped `P_TryWalk` at tic 77 of demo 3; not yet localised). A **separate bug** crashes any second-WAD or external-lump load (`index out of range [1264] with length 1` in the lump loader), so random demos are fed to gore by replacing the DEMO1 lump inside an IWAD copy (`make_demo_iwad.py`).
* **Structure versus behaviour:** the structural matcher pairs `p_CrossSubsector` with `P_CrossSubsector` (extension tier 2, name match) and traces about 45% of gore's functions to `linuxdoom`; it cannot see that the function is wrong, because the bug leaves the function's shape essentially unchanged. Structure locates counterparts; behaviour tests them.
* gore's own tests compare rendered screenshots, not simulation sync, which is consistent with these bugs going unnoticed.

## Third: room4doom (Rust; a hand rewrite of the C source, not a machine translation)
Rust 1.100.0 and alsa-lib installed in the private prefix; built from a patched copy (`demo/harness/build_room4doom.py`, the repo's own `--headless --iwad ... --demo demo1` mode, our digest after its per-tic trace call). The repo's `software` feature does not compile at this commit (a signature mismatch in its window backend), so the default feature set was built; the digest needs no window. The README claims "complete demo compatibility with OG Doom (no longtics yet)".
* **Shareware demos 1 to 3:** identical to the reference on position, view angle (outside death), health, armor, weapon, the gameplay RNG and three of four ammo types at **every tic**. The only fields that ever differ are the view angle after death and **bullets (`ammo0`)**: from the first dropped-clip pickup (tics 735, 176, 406) `room4doom` is 10 bullets ahead (+15 where the reference gains +5). The trajectory is unaffected.
* **300 random self-play demos** (`demo/phase0_room4doom_vs_chocolate_random300.csv`): only **45 gameplay-identical** (15%; 36 strictly), 255 diverge, median first divergence tic 18. In **117 of them it is exactly tic 18**, the first pistol shot: with the fire button held from the start, the reference fires at tic 18 and room4doom at tic 19 (a one-tic offset in the initial weapon raise, which human demos never reach because nobody presses fire that early).
* **Controlled test (post hoc, flagged):** 150 demos with the buttons forced off for the first 40 tics (`..._quietstart150.csv`): 58 gameplay-identical (39%), no divergence at tic 18, median first divergence now tic 1,172. The weapon-raise timing explains the early cluster; later divergences (RNG index, ammo) are not yet explained.
* So a hand rewrite that claims full demo compatibility reproduces human demos almost exactly, yet random self-play finds timing and pickup differences the stock demos never exercise.

## Stronger tests, round 1 (2026-11-26): wider digest and illegal-input demos
* **Wider digest:** a fingerprint of the whole world each tic (every object and monster's type, position, angle, momentum and health, combined without regard to list order; every sector's floor, ceiling and special; the console player's own object excluded because it has its own columns). Implemented in the reference, Mocha and gore; not in room4doom (its own trace uses a different format). Light level is left out on purpose: it only affects rendering. (Side finding: Mocha's light level differs by 3 from the C engines in 2 of 143 sectors of the first map from the first tic; cosmetic.)
* **Effect on Mocha, 300 sticky random demos:** 290 gameplay-identical (was 291 on the player-only digest): the wider digest finds about one extra latent divergence per 300. Example: on shareware demo 1 an object's state differs from tic 1,673 in three short windows (26, 26 and 14 tics) while the player state is identical, then re-converges.
* **Chaos demos** (`gen_demos.py ... chaos`: fresh full-range random bytes every tic: forward -127..127, strafe -128..127, turn 0..255, buttons 0..0x7F; 200 demos of 3,000 tics): **the six C engines agree on 200 of 200.** (A first version let forward = -128 through, which is the demo-end marker; the reference stopped early and Mocha played on. That is a genuine small difference, Mocha ignores a mid-demo end marker, and it was excluded from the corrected generator.)
* **Translations and the rewrite on chaos input** (`demo/phase1_*.csv`): Mocha 191 of 200 gameplay-identical (36 strictly; angle-only differences while dead in 163), consistent with its sticky-demo rate; room4doom **1 of 200** (the first divergence is the weapon in 131 demos, from about tic 29: rapid random weapon-switch requests are handled differently); gore **0 of 200**.
* **Not yet done:** coverage-guided demo generation (aim demos at code the random ones miss; needs gcov on the reference) and goal-directed play (use doors, keys, secrets, finish levels); wider digest for room4doom; Doom 2 content and multiplayer modes.

## What this means for the studies
* **Study 1 as first framed has little to explain among established C ports:** sync does not vary among them on shareware or random demos. The outcome variance sits elsewhere (non-vanilla-exact engines like `puredoom`, translations, ports in non-compatible modes), so study 1 should be re-scoped there, with translations as the main case.
* **Performance (study 2) and the call-count oracle (study 3) do not depend on sync variance.** Speed certainly varies; per-function call counts are language-independent, and demos are now a cheap, reproducible driver for both.
* **Not yet built:** `dsda-doom` (needs OpenGL), `woof` and `nugget-doom` (need SDL3 3.4+ and OpenAL), and the other translations (`managed-doom` C#, `doom-scheme`, `dartdoom`, `kocoa-doom` Kotlin, `doo-cr` Crystal).

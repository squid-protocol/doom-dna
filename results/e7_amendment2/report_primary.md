## Scorecard against the registered predictions (Amendment 1 §F)
- **H7.1** (structure closer to the name-blind text arbiter in at least 75% of same-language ports): 21/22 = 95% on exact+near. Met. Caveat: names rank the ports about as well or better than structure (Spearman vs text above); structure is better calibrated, names overstate.
- **H7.2** (descriptive, Spearman at least 0.80): point estimate 0.778, cluster-bootstrap interval includes values below 0.80.
- **H7.3** (descriptive, at least a third of cross-language ports that structure leaves undetected attach to a declared family member under names): 2/5 = 40%.
- **H7.4** (primary; structure 0 and names at least 1 false attachments among 5 large unrelated controls): structure 1, names 1. **Structure prediction not met as registered**: the one attachment is `lua` to `srb2`, and SRB2 vendors Lua (`src/blua/`), so it is a genuine copy kept by the registered vendor policy, not noise.
- **H7.5a** (primary; M1 corrected above the within-language null's 95th percentile): structure 0.560 vs 0.240; names 0.640 vs 0.240. Met for both.

## Known defects of the registered rules found in Phase A (to be handled by a dated amendment, not by changing these numbers)
1. **M8 'generic key' rule.** A key present in at least 10 of the 51 map programs is called generic, but 33 of the 51 are DOOM ports, so DOOM's own ancestral vocabulary is labelled generic and rule 1 fires on every DOOM pair; rules 2 to 5 are never reached.
2. **No de-duplication of eligible functions.** See the diagnostic above.
3. **Vendored libraries are kept** (registered), so real vendored code (Lua in SRB2) counts as an attachment.

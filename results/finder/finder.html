DOOM Function Finder

Pick a function from linuxdoom (the 1997 id Software source) and see what each of 26 DOOM ports did with it. Every match was made without names, from each function's shape and its place in the call graph, and is tagged with which route found it, so you can see how far to trust it. The seven tiers, and how often each is confirmed

In same-language ports, the share of matches that a separate text comparison also confirmed (a floor: "not listed" means the comparison did not list that pair, not that it is wrong). The text comparison never chooses a match.

What you can and cannot conclude

  • Exact means identical structural fingerprint, not identical text. Unrelated programs still produce a few exact pairs on very short functions (8 to 12 lines).
  • Seeds are precise call-graph matches across languages (Go, Scheme, Dart). Extension adds reach, not accuracy: it keeps producing matches whether or not there is anything to find, so treat hop 2 and 3 as leads.
  • The gate is a coherence check on a whole port's extension. A failed gate means do not trust extension matches for that port. A passed gate does not mean they are right (managed-doom passes, and its matches are almost all wrong).
  • C# ports (managed-doom, kocoa-doom): neither route finds them. "No match" there means unsolved, not "kept nothing".
  • Translation checks use function-name agreement, which misses ports that renamed their functions: it is a lower bound. A blind audit by model readers confirmed 80 of 80 name-agreeing pairs.
  • Ports above 10,000 functions (gzdoom, doomsday, doom3-bfg) are not covered. Nothing here measures whether architecture was preserved.

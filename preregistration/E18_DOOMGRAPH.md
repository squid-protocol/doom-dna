# E18 (DRAFT v1, 2026-11-27): DoomGraph v0, a cross-port meta layer, and what it can predict

**Status: DRAFT; two independent reviews and a dated reconciliation before any hold-out score is computed.** Schema: `doomgraph/SCHEMA.md`.

## Question
Can the layers we already hold for Doom's ports (structure, matches, lineage, call counts, lesion phenotypes) be merged into one graph of **ortholog groups** such that holding out one layer for one port and predicting it from the rest works well? The prediction accuracy is the measurable value of the graph; a database whose layers do not predict each other would be a catalogue, not a model.

## Build (deterministic, versioned)
* Instances from the scans; match edges from the E12 pair files, name-agree and E7 text-arbiter edges; **ortholog groups** by reference-anchored clustering (an instance joins the group of its best-scoring anchor in the reference engines when the evidence chain is consistent, with conflicts and many-to-many cases kept as flagged group members, never silently merged). The clustering rule and confidence thresholds are frozen by commit before any hold-out score is computed.
* Layers ingested as in the schema, with the E15 hot/inert masks verbatim. **Mocha and gore lesion outcomes are excluded** until E17's analysis is frozen and read.

## Hold-out tasks (each leave-one-port-out; the held-out port's own layer value is removed from the graph before prediction)
* **P1 (primary): phenotype from the neighbours.** For chocolate-doom, managed-doom and prboom-plus functions, predict the function's lesion-effect family profile and its hot/inert class from the **same ortholog group's phenotypes in the other screened ports** plus its call-graph neighbours. Score: agreement with the actual phenotype (the E15 distance D to the held-out truth; top-5 recovery of the function among group candidates; hot/inert accuracy and AUC). Baselines: group-size prior, name-only partner phenotype, the call-count profile nearest neighbour, and a random-group control.
* **P2: edge validity.** Predict whether a match edge is behaviourally confirmed from structural features, tier, neighbourhood agreement and group context (the SFS confidence score is the baseline to beat), scored by AUC within tier.
* **P3: link prediction.** Hide 10% of match edges between a port and its reference; recover them from the rest of the graph (group evidence and call-graph neighbourhood); score hit@1 and hit@5 against random and name-agree baselines.
* **P4 (descriptive): conservation.** Report per ortholog group: the number of ports present, divergence, lineage depth and behavioural conservation, and the share of groups with at least one behaviourally divergent instance.

## Thresholds (fixed now)
* **P1 passes** if the held-out family profile is within the E15 5th-percentile distance of the truth for at least **30 percentage points more** of the functions than the best baseline, with a cluster-bootstrap (clusters = ortholog groups) lower bound above 0 and at least **60** groups scored.
* **P2/P3** are reported against their baselines with intervals; no pass/fail beyond a stated lift of 0.05 AUC for P2.
* **Uninterpretable:** fewer than 60 scorable groups, group construction instability (membership changing by more than 10% under a different anchor), hold-out leakage found in an audit, or results carried by one subsystem.

## Controls
Shuffled group labels; hold-out of a different layer to confirm no label leakage; an anchor-swap (chocolate-doom vs linuxdoom); a leave-lineage-out version (hold out a whole fork family) because forks share code.

## Limits
One program; groups are built from the same match evidence the baselines use, so P3 is partly circular and is reported as such; faithful ports dominate the screened layers (E17 adds divergent ones later); behavioural truth exists only for screened ports.

## v2 (2026-11-27): reconciled after two independent reviews (Claude subagent; Gemini, `results/e16/E18_review_gemini.txt`). Where v2 differs from v1, v2 governs.
**Reframing (both reviews).** Only three ports have lesion screens (chocolate-doom, managed-doom, prboom-plus); two are near-clone C forks, and E15 A4 already showed name and call-count baselines are near-ceiling on these faithful pairs, so v1's "+30 points over the best baseline" is unsatisfiable and P1 on three ports is largely "copy the twin's phenotype". Therefore: **P1 on the three screened ports is a retrospective development benchmark, not a confirmatory test** (E15 and E16 outcomes for these ports are known). The confirmatory P1 is **deferred** until the blind divergent-port screens (Mocha, then gore; E17) are analysed and ingested, and it will be registered then with thresholds calibrated by simulation (donor noise, group size) as in E15. What v0 delivers confirmatorily is the **static graph build** (structure, name, text, lineage evidence only) and its audits.
**Static-only build boundary (hard rule, enforced by construction).** Ortholog groups are built in a **separate database file** that contains only instances, match edges, call edges and lineage edges; the behaviour tables (reach, lesions, phenotypes, conformance) live in a second file and are never attached to the group-building code. An automated audit checks that no phenotype, reach or behavioural quantity appears in any `membership.evidence` field, and a canary null-label audit confirms no layer leaks across the boundary.
**Group construction spec (frozen by commit before any hold-out score).** Anchor: **linuxdoom** (the ancestor, never a test port); an anchor-swap run on chocolate-doom is reported as a stability check, per anchor and never as a single pass/fail. Edge weights: tier score ordering F0 > F1c > S > ext tier 1 > 2 > 3, name-agree as a separate evidence channel, text-arbiter as confirmation; a non-anchor instance joins the group of its best-weighted anchor if the evidence chain is consistent (no path longer than 2 hops, deterministic port order, fixed tie-breakers). Splits (one port function matching several reference functions), merges (several port functions to one) and conflicts are **explicit member roles** (`split`, `merge`, `conflict`) and are **excluded from P1 scoring** (reported as counts). Ungrouped functions are reported and scored as misses in any group-dependent metric. Pair-file hashes, scan commit per port, name-stripping rules and seeds are recorded in `build_manifest`.
**Development benchmark P1 (descriptive).** One primary scalar: the macro-averaged share of held-out functions whose phenotype distance D to the group-mate donor's phenotype is at or below the E15 5th-percentile cutoff; hot and inert functions are predicted without the held-out port's masks; ungrouped functions are misses; **missing D (fewer than 5 co-observed demos) counts as a miss for the graph and the baselines alike**. Split by **donor type** (code twin present, translation only, no donor) and **excluding identical-text pairs** from the headline; leave-lineage-out is the headline condition. Baselines: **direct pairwise E12 match without the graph**, name-only, **call-count nearest neighbour (headline comparator)**, same-file and same-subsystem phenotype priors, size- and reach-matched priors, ortholog-only versus neighbour-only ablations, a covariate model on size, reach and degree, and the hot/inert base rate per subsystem. Bootstrap clusters = source file and subsystem; Holm over secondary metrics; D, top-5 and hot/inert accuracy secondary.
**P2 (descriptive).** Label = E15 behavioural confirmation; features exclude any call-profile-derived quantity; `D`-only confirmation is evaluated separately from the call-profile clause; within-tier AUC with an endpoint-clustered bootstrap; the 3-port P2 trains on one port pair and is exploratory.
**P3 (descriptive).** Groups are **rebuilt inside each fold** with all parallel edges on hidden pairs stripped (name-agree, text-arbiter, sibling E12 tiers, 2-hop clone paths); the 10% hidden set is stratified by tier and fixed (seeds and folds stored in the database) before any score; hit@1 and hit@5 reported per tier.
**Uninterpretable gates (pre-declared).** Anchor-swap ARI < 0.85 on shared functions; ungrouped rate > 30%; missing-D > 40%; the best baseline > 70% (ceiling, nothing to add); fewer than 60 groups with a usable donor and a usable target; results carried by one subsystem supplying more than 40% of scored groups; any audit failure. **Controls:** shuffles stratified by size, reach and subsystem; a true-negative (no-ortholog) control; the canary leakage audit; a positive control on planted phenotypes in a mock store.
**Schema v1 (changes to SCHEMA.md).** Primary and foreign keys; surrogate `edge_id` and canonical undirected ordering; `build_manifest` (code commit, pair-file hashes, scan hash and scanner version per port, seeds); `instance_key` = hash of (port, file, name, signature) alongside `fid`; `match_edge.gate_passed`; `membership.role` and `build_scope`; decomposed `behaviour_confirmed` (D, threshold, call-profile flag); `sfs_confidence` formula version; `demo` table (IWAD, family); `phenotype` records the E15 pipeline commit and demo-set id; `layer_availability(port, layer, n_instances)`; `lineage_edge` only (no `port.lineage_parent`); a `holdout_fold` table; `evidence` fields are structured IDs and enums only (no free text, no snippets, no text-arbiter rationales), `scan_path` stored relative or hashed, and an ingest allowlist test fails the build on any text column over a length bound or any source-like content.

## Build v0 result and amendment A1 (2026-11-27, before any hold-out score)
**Build v0 (anchor linuxdoom, rules as in v2) trips two pre-declared gates:** ungrouped rate 0.743 over all 106,803 instances (gate 0.30) and anchor-swap ARI 0.514 on shared functions (gate 0.85). Everything else passed (14 of 14 integrity audits: no behaviour quantity in membership evidence, canary 0 hits, groups reproduce from stored evidence, no long text). Causes seen in the build report: linuxdoom has only 779 functions, so most functions in large engines have no ancestor; merges (12,436) and conflicts (2,286) dominate the roles, and ARI rises to 0.858 only for functions that are plain members in both runs. Among **screened-port** functions the ungrouped counts are chocolate-doom 0 of 199, managed-doom 65 of 250, prboom-plus 6 of 226 (71 of 675, 10.5%). **v0 is therefore labelled uninterpretable for confirmatory use as the gates were written; it stays available for descriptive use only.**
**Amendment A1 (fixed now, before the v1 build is looked at):** (1) the gates are **not** relaxed: anchor-swap ARI >= 0.85 on all shared grouped functions stays; the ARI over plain members is reported alongside, not as a substitute; (2) the **ungrouped-rate gate applies to the evaluation population** (simulation-scope functions of the screened ports, the only population any hold-out score uses), with the full-population rate reported descriptively; the v0 value on that population is 10.5%, which passes, but v0 still fails the ARI gate; (3) build **v1** replaces the single linuxdoom anchor with a **multi-anchor consensus** (the five reference engines linuxdoom, chocolate-doom, doomgeneric, prboom-plus, woof; a function joins a group only when anchors agree, conflicts explicit), keeps the static-only boundary, and must pass **both** gates on its own, otherwise the graph stays descriptive and no hold-out benchmark is run. The failure of v0 and this amendment are disclosed in any report.
